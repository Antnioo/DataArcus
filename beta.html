DataArcus for Power BI

It checks your Power BI model, shows you a plan, waits for your go, and writes a new report as a Power BI project you open in Desktop. In English, or in Arabic right to left. It works next to Microsoft's tools, inside Claude Desktop, on your own laptop.

What it does today

Everything in this list works in the current build. It is still a beta, so tell us where it breaks.

  • Reads your model's structure, not its data: tables, columns, measures, their types and formats, from a Power BI project (.pbip), a model.bim or a template (.pbit). On a large model it gives a summary first, then the tables you name.
  • Checks the model before it builds: a score, and findings that name the objects concerned, such as unused or broken fields, months that would sort A to Z, measures without a format and numbers without a thousand separator. Fixes come as a ready script file that you apply yourself in Power BI Desktop.
  • Checks the calendar against the Gulf: Hijri, Ramadan and Eid columns, and the weekend of each Gulf country. For a calendar made with our Calendar Generator, it compares its Ramadan and Eid dates with the announced ones (UAE and Saudi Arabia).
  • Shows you a plan and waits for your go: the pages, the visuals, the fields on each, the sizes and the theme. Nothing is written before you agree, and it binds exactly the fields you approved.
  • Writes a new report next to your model: KPI cards, charts, a table, slicers, a tooltip page, page buttons, a filter rail or a slide-in filter panel, and a phone layout, with text and button sizes measured in Power BI Desktop.
  • Arabic right to left: the page is mirrored and the page buttons follow the reading order. Arabic field names are the ones you give; it never translates your model's names by itself.
  • Numbers that read well: KPI cards with a thousand separator (101,914, not 101.914K), and a theme with its colour contrast checked.
  • Part of the data when you ask: page filters such as "Ramadan only", or one Ramadan by its Hijri year.
  • Careful with your files: it never changes your model, never overwrites or deletes a file, and works only inside the one folder you choose. It points out names that hide invisible characters.
  • Checked by Microsoft's own validator: on our test models, Microsoft's report validator finds 0 errors in the reports it writes.

Experimental

Picture columns in a table: a small SVG picture on every row (a progress bar, a ring, an arrow or a label), drawn from a measure that lives only in the report, so your model is not touched. It works, and we are still tuning how it looks.

Planned, not in the beta yet

  • Planned: the number check. The same values in every visual before and after a change, compared on your machine.
  • Planned: check any report. Microsoft's validator and our measured rules on any report, including one made with Microsoft's tools or Copilot.
  • Planned: edit a report. A new copy of an existing report with your changes; the original is never overwritten.
  • Planned: add a Gulf calendar. The Calendar Generator inside the tool: a script file that adds a calendar table with Hijri dates, Ramadan, Eid and your country's weekend.

Your data, in plain words

  • It runs on your computer and sends nothing anywhere by itself: no account, no tracking.
  • What its tools answer is read by your AI app; with Claude, that goes to Anthropic under your Claude account's terms. Those answers hold your model's structure (names, types and formats), never the data in your tables.
  • It doesn't connect to Power BI Desktop or to your data sources, and it runs no queries.
  • Names in a model are treated as data, never as instructions.
  • Use only a model whose structure you may share with an AI service. The full statement is in our privacy note for the tool, and it comes with the package.

What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 laptop (the beta is Windows only).
  • Power BI Desktop, a recent version (we test on 2.158).
  • Claude Desktop, installed and signed in.
  • A Power BI model you are allowed to use for testing, saved as a Power BI project (.pbip).
