Power BI Pro, Premium Per User or Fabric: Which License Do You Actually Need?

Posted on September 23, 2026

Licensing is the question that comes up most before a Power BI project starts. Most companies either overpay for capacity they do not need, or keep buying Pro seats long after capacity became cheaper. The answer usually depends on one number: how many people only view reports.

The three options in one minute

  • Power BI Pro ($14 per user per month). Everyone who publishes, shares or views content in a shared workspace needs a license. Covers most small and mid-size teams.
  • Premium Per User ($24 per user per month). Everything in Pro plus Premium features: larger models, up to 48 scheduled refreshes a day, deployment pipelines and XMLA read and write. The catch: every person who views PPU content also needs PPU.
  • Fabric capacity (F SKUs). You pay for compute by the hour instead of per person: $0.18 per capacity unit per hour pay-as-you-go in US regions, about 40.5% less with a one-year reservation. From F64 up, viewers with a free license can open reports. Below F64, viewers still need Pro. Report creators always need Pro.

The number that decides it: viewers

An F64 costs about $8,410 a month pay-as-you-go, or about $5,004 a month with a one-year reservation. Pro costs $14 per viewer. Divide one by the other and you get the break-even point: a reserved F64 pays for itself at about 358 viewers, and pay-as-you-go at about 601 viewers.

The number of creators does not move this point, because creators need Pro in both setups. Below it, Pro for everyone is cheaper. Above it, F64 wins, and the gap grows with every viewer you add.

A worked example

A dealership group has 12 analysts building reports and 200 managers and sales staff viewing them. Pro for everyone: 212 × $14 = $2,968 a month. A reserved F64 plus 12 Pro seats: $5,004 + $168 = $5,172 a month. Pro wins by more than $2,000.

Now the group rolls the dashboards out to every branch and reaches 450 viewers. Pro for everyone: 462 × $14 = $6,468 a month. The F64 setup stays at $5,172. Capacity now saves about $1,300 a month, and every new branch makes it cheaper per person.

When the cheapest option is not the right one

  • You need Fabric workloads. Lakehouses, pipelines and notebooks only run on capacity. A small F2 plus Pro seats is often the cheapest way in.
  • Your models are outgrowing Pro. Pro caps model size at 1 GB and scheduled refresh at 8 times a day. PPU or capacity removes both limits.
  • You have Microsoft 365 E5. E5 already includes Pro, so Pro costs you nothing extra and PPU becomes an add-on of about $10 per user.
  • You want to show reports to customers. Embedding reports in your own app or portal needs capacity, not per user licenses.
